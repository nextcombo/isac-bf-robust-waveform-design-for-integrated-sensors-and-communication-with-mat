# ISAC+BF: Robust Waveform Design for Integrated Sensors and Communication [with MATLAB Code]

Integrated Sensor and Communications (ISAC) technology enables the sharing of hardware, resources (such as spectrum), and waveforms, becoming a key feature of next-generation communication systems. This paper investigates the performance characterization and waveform design issues of ISAC systems under conditions where the actual communication channels are not accurately known. Due to the uncertainty in the nominal communication channels, the Pareto frontier for the nominal sensor and communications performance cannot represent the real-world trade-offs of the ISAC system. Therefore, this paper presents a robust (i.e., conservative) Pareto frontier that considers the uncertainty in the communication channels. Specifically, by exploring the problem of robust waveform design, we aim to find the lower bound of the true but unknown Pareto frontier, which seeks to optimize the waveform in the worst-case scenario within the channel. The problem of robust waveform design studied in this paper is proved to be nonconvex and high-dimensional, making it intractable with existing optimization techniques. To address this, we propose a computationally efficient solution framework to approximate these problems. Simulation results indicate that solving the robust waveform design problem can yield an approximation of the true but unknown Pareto frontier, which characterizes the trade-off between sensor and communication performance under communication channel uncertainty.
Introduction Integrated Sensor and Communications (ISAC) is one of the enabling technologies for the sixth generation (6G) communication. It utilizes a single hardware system to simultaneously perform sensor and communication functions, enhancing spectral efficiency, minimizing platform size, and controlling power consumption. From a signal processing perspective, one of the main characteristics of ISAC is the use of a dual-function waveform to achieve both sensor and communication functions using the same transmission waveform. This paper focuses on the performance characterization and waveform design of ISAC systems.
Performance characterization and waveform design: The ISAC's performance characterization encompasses two aspects: first, evaluating the ISAC system's performance in terms of sensing and communication under given dual-function waveforms; second, exploring performance boundaries by depicting the Pareto frontier of sensing and communication. Typical performance metrics for sensing include the Cramér-Rao lower bound (CRLB) for estimated parameters, and the sensing mutual information between the received signal and the sensing channel (i.e., the sensing rate). Typical performance metrics for communication include the minimum mean squared error (MMSE) for distortion and the mutual information between transmitted and received signals (i.e., the channel capacity). As a technical aspect of waveform design for ISAC, its aim is to depict the Pareto frontier by finding optimal dual-function waveforms at different trade-off levels, as the optimal waveform for sensing may not necessarily be optimal for communication, and vice versa. Assuming an ISAC system aims to minimize sensing metrics while maximizing communication metrics, such as the CRLB for sensing and the achievable and rate for communication, its performance Pareto frontier is shown in Figure 1. Each point on the Pareto frontier is associated with at least one optimal dual-function waveform that balances the performance between sensing and communication. Based on performance priorities and underlying system and signal characteristics, existing ISAC waveform design methods can be categorized into three types: waveform design centered on communication, ensuring nearly perfect communication performance, with secondary optimization of sensing performance; waveform design centered on sensing, ensuring nearly perfect sensing performance, with secondary optimization of communication performance; and joint waveform design, aimed at completely balancing communication and sensing performance, eliminating inherent performance limitations of both. From the design method perspective, existing methods can also be divided into two main streams: one is to directly design dual-function waveforms through optimization and constraints of various performance metrics; the other is to find balanced waveforms by matching them with known benchmark waveforms.
Contribution: This paper proposes a robust dual-function waveform design framework to counteract communication channel uncertainty; it introduces the concept of ISAC performance characterization under channel uncertainty, indicating that seeking the lower bound (i.e., conservative performance boundary) of the true Pareto front is related to solving the robust waveform design problem; to address the two-layered min-max robust waveform design problem, an approximate solution framework is proposed. The first two contributions are novel in the ISAC field, while the third contribution enriches general optimization theory and practice (such as robustness theory, game theory, etc.).
